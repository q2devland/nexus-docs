List views

A List view is the grid of a Collection’s Items. Picking a Collection in the Content section’s Navigation tree opens it, but for a singleton, such as Reelhouse’s homepage, which opens its one Item instead. Each row is an Item; pressing it, or Enter on it, opens it in the Item view.

Its header counts the Items it keeps, holds the search box and the buttons for Filters, Columns, Export to CSV and the Saved view menu, and New Item (+) when your Role may create the Collection’s Items. Where the window is too narrow for them, they’re in a More actions menu.

When the Collection has no Items yet, the grid says No rows to show. under a short animation. When the search or the filters keep none, it says No Items match. instead, with a Clear search and filters button that shows every Item again. The animation holds still when your system asks for reduced motion, and is left out of a grid too short for it. Every list in Nexus does the same, as a grid or as cards, such as the File library, Users, the Delivery log and History, each in its own words: No files match., No changes match. and so on, the button clearing only the filters where there is no search.

Searching

Section titled “Searching”

The search box looks in the Collection’s Searchable fields, and keeps the Items with a text one that contains what you typed, whatever the case and accents of either, or a number one that is it. Searching Reelhouse’s films for comet finds Paper Comets; searching its people for penafiel finds Hugo Peñafiel.

When the Collection’s Translations collection has Searchable fields, the search looks in their Translations too, in the Content language the List view shows: in Spanish, cometas finds Paper Comets, translated as Cometas de papel. A Collection with no Searchable fields has no search box: reach its Items through the filters instead.

Filtering

Section titled “Filtering”

Filters opens the filters panel over the List view, which keeps the Items that pass every filter. Each filter takes:

  • a Field, picked from a tree of the Collection’s Fields, which opens any Relation into the Fields of the Collection it leads to, at any depth: a film’s genres by their name, or its cast by the year they were born; and its Translations into their Fields, read in the Content language the List view shows. A date Field opens into its Value and its parts, such as its Year or its Weekday, and a many-relation offers its Count, how many Items it links: see Field functions;
  • an Operator, which depends on the Field: is, contains, starts with and is empty for texts, is at least and is less than for numbers, is before and is on or after for dates, contains any of and contains all of for lists, and more;
  • a Value, typed or picked: the Items of a Relation are chosen from that Collection’s List view, and a month, a weekday or a quarter from their names. Texts may Match case and accents, or not.

Filters apply as you change them, and keep applying once the panel closes: its button says how many are active. Remove all clears them.

A filter on a film’s Translations › Title names no language: it reads the Translation in the Content language the List view shows, and picking another reads that one instead. In Spanish, starts with cometas keeps Paper Comets and Kites over Karsk, translated as Cometas de papel and Cometas sobre Karsk; in English, it keeps none. An Item without a Translation in that language has its translated Fields empty: is empty and is null keep it, and no other operator does, is not and does not contain included.

Sorting

Section titled “Sorting”

A column’s header menu sorts the Items by it, Sort ascending or Sort descending, or, once they’re sorted by another column, Then by ascending or Then by descending, to order those the first sort leaves equal: Reelhouse’s films by certification, then by release year. Clear sort stops sorting by that column. Columns that hold several values, through a many-relation Field, and Repeaters don’t sort. A column of a date’s part sorts by its number: a month’s from January to December, whatever language it’s written in.

Columns

Section titled “Columns”

A List view starts with the id, the Collection’s own Fields in their order, and when its Items were created and updated. Fields that store nothing, such as Notices, and One to Many, Many to Many and Many to Any Fields are left out, but can be added.

  • Add column, in the Columns panel or a header’s menu, adds a Field picked from the same tree as the filters: a season’s TV show by its title, an episode’s season’s TV show, or a film’s Translation, in the Content language the List view shows. A column through a many-relation Field shows the first few of its values and how many more there are.
  • A date Field opens in that tree into its Value and its parts: Release date · Month is a column of the month the films opened in, written by name in your UI language, as a weekday is; the other parts are numbers. A timestamp’s are read in your Profile’s timezone.
  • Move left and Move right in a header’s menu, dragging a header, or the Columns panel’s arrows and handles put them in order.
  • Hide column takes one away, and Fit to content sizes it to its values; dragging a header’s edge sizes it too.
  • Reset, in the Columns panel, brings back the Collection’s own columns.

Paging

Section titled “Paging”

As the Project settings say, a List view either scrolls through all its Items, loading more as you go, or shows a page at a time, with a page selector at its foot and a choice of 25, 50 or 100 rows a page.

Besides the first, previous, next and last page buttons, you can type the page to go to in the box between them, then press Enter or leave the box. A number below the first page goes to the first, one above the last goes to the last, and anything that isn’t a whole number puts the current page back. The File library and the other paged lists work the same way.

Content language

Section titled “Content language”

When a Collection has Translations, the List view shows and searches them in one Content language: the default one, until you pick another in its Content language menu, which marks the mandatory ones. It offers the menu too when a column’s Items show by a Display template that shows their Translations, which read in that language; the Collection’s Item view shows its title and its Relations’ Items in it too. See Translating Items.

What the List view remembers

Section titled “What the List view remembers”

Each List view keeps, in your browser, how you left it: its search, filters, columns and their widths, sort, page size and Content language, until you change them. It’s a convenience for you alone, on that browser: to keep a way of showing the Items, name it, or share it, save it as a Saved view.

Selecting and deleting

Section titled “Selecting and deleting”

The checkboxes at the start of each row select Items, and the one in the header selects all those shown. While any is selected, the header turns into a selection bar: how many are selected, the Actions you may run on them, Export selected to CSV and, if your Role may delete them, Delete selected.

Deleting them, once confirmed, deletes each as deleting it alone would. Those that other Items refer to through a Many to One that refuses the deletion, or that an Extension refuses, stay, selected, with the reason.

Exporting to CSV

Section titled “Exporting to CSV”

Export to CSV opens a dialog with copies of what the List view shows: its columns, search, filters and sort, which you change there for this export only. Its CSV header shows the first line of the file, the keys of the columns.

  • Columns: added, removed and ordered as the List view’s are. A column of a date’s part is written as its number, 3 for March, a timestamp’s read in your Profile’s timezone.
  • Filters and Search: as the List view’s, until changed here, a timestamp’s parts read in your Profile’s timezone.
  • Content languages: a column of Translations is written once for each language ticked, the List view’s at first.

Export downloads every Item they keep, however many, as a file named after the Collection’s key, films.csv. Exported from a selection, it holds only the Items selected. The file is UTF-8, its values as the List view shows them; a Repeater’s Rows are written as JSON, and a value a spreadsheet would take for a formula starts with ', so that opening the file runs nothing an Item holds.

Who sees what

Section titled “Who sees what”

A List view shows only the Collections, Items and Fields your Role may read: a Field hidden from it is no column and no filter, and a Relation leading to a Collection it can’t read can’t be followed. See Collection permissions and Field permissions.
