Entries

Each Item of a Collection that keeps a Read model is one entry of it, which a client reads as JSON. The Saltmarsh Inheritance, read in Spanish:

What every entry has

Section titled “What every entry has”
  • id: the Collection’s key, a hyphen and the Item’s id, such as films-2, unique across Collections. It’s what Relations hold and what a client reads an entry by.
  • collection: its Collection’s key.
  • itemId: the Item’s own id, as text, as the Items API knows it.
  • updatedOn: when the Item last changed, in ISO 8601, in UTC: the change’s time, not the rebuild’s. A change to the model that rebuilds the entry leaves it as it was. The Saltmarsh Inheritance’s is when Marta Quiroga corrected its runtime.

Then come its chosen Fields, by key, but the id, already there; a chosen Field named id, collection, itemId or updatedOn is hidden by them. A Field’s value is as the Items API answers it: a number as a number, a date as 2021-03-12, a Dropdown as its value, a WYSIWYG as its HTML, an empty Field as null.

An entry holds no Display text: the Display template is how the CMS names an Item to its Users. A client composes what it shows from the Fields it chose: Reelhouse’s website would show The Saltmarsh Inheritance by its title and release_year, as La herencia de la marisma (2021), in the language it reads.

Relations

Section titled “Relations”

A Relation holds the ids of the entries it leads to:

  • A Many to One, one id or null: an episode’s season is "seasons-1".
  • A One to Many, the ids of its Items, in their order when sorted: a season’s episodes.
  • A Many to Many, those of the Items it links, in the order of its links when it’s sorted, else of the linked Items’ ids: a film’s genres are ["genres-1", "genres-5"], Drama and Mystery.
  • A Many to Any, those of the Items it links of the Collections that keep a Read model, in its order: the homepage’s featured holds films and TV shows, ["films-12", "tv_shows-3", …].

A Many to Many or Many to Any whose Junction collection has Fields of its own holds the ids of its links instead: a film’s cast holds films_cast entries, each with the character played and the billing_order, and the person it links. See Including relations, which loads related entries in place of their ids.

Files

Section titled “Files”

A File or Image Field holds its File’s metadata, or null:

  • id: the File’s id.
  • url: where Nexus serves its bytes, a path of the Nexus that serves it, read with the same authentication as the API.
  • filename: its name as uploaded; title: its title in the File library.
  • type: its MIME type; size: its bytes.
  • width and height, of an image alone, in pixels.

Replacing or retitling a File rebuilds every entry that holds it, so its metadata is never stale.

Image URLs

Section titled “Image URLs”

An Image URL holds its URL as entered and its name, or null: Paper Comets’ still is { "url": "films/paper-comets.png", "name": "paper-comets" }. The URL isn’t resolved against the Field’s base URL, and its URL previews aren’t listed: they are the CMS’s alone. A client resolves it against the base URL it knows, https://img.reelhouse.example/stills/ for Reelhouse.

Video keys

Section titled “Video keys”

A Video key holds its site, its key and its name, or null: Paper Comets’ trailer is { "site": "reelhouse-player", "key": "paper-comets", "name": "Paper Comets: trailer" }. The site is the Video site’s identifier, and the embed URL isn’t there: a client plays each site its own way.

Translations

Section titled “Translations”

A Project with Content languages reads every entry in one of them. An entry’s chosen translated Fields in that language are merged into it, taking the place of its own Fields of the same key: The Saltmarsh Inheritance’s title is La herencia de la marisma in Spanish and The Saltmarsh Inheritance in English, and its synopsis, which only its Translations hold, is in the language read.

A translated Relation holds, in each language, the ids its translation in it leads to, as an own Relation would: The Saltmarsh Inheritance’s voice_cast, who dub it, holds the links of its two Spanish voices in Spanish, and is [] in English, the language it was shot in. See Including relations.

An Item without a translation in the language read has none of its translated Fields, its translated Relations either, which a client falls back from as it sees fit; its own Fields of the same key are still there. Every one of Reelhouse’s films is translated into Spanish, and its TV shows, which have no Translations, read the same in either language.

A Content language created later gives every entry its place in it at once, empty until its Items are translated.
