The Reelhouse demo

Reelhouse is an invented film and TV catalogue, and every example, screenshot and API sample on this site comes from it. It’s a real Project: a Model snapshot and the Items seeded into it, which you can run on your own machine and change as you read. Every title, person, character and picture in it is made up.

It holds 12 films with their Spanish translations, 4 TV shows with 8 seasons and 30 episodes, 30 people and the 51 characters they play, in 8 genres.

Its model

Section titled “Its model”

Admins see the Collections in the Data model section, arranged in the Navigation tree under two Folders: Catalogue, with films, TV shows, their seasons and episodes, and genres, and People, with people and characters. The homepage leads the tree, and the Content languages collection, languages, closes it, outside any Folder.

What each Collection shows off

Section titled “What each Collection shows off”

Between them, Reelhouse’s Collections use every concept the rest of this site explains, so each page has something to point at.

Collection What it shows off
films An Image poster, in the Posters File folder; a still, an Image URL on Reelhouse’s image server, https://img.reelhouse.example/stills/, with URL previews 160 and 480 px wide; a trailer, a Video key on Reelhouse’s video player, keyed by the film’s slug; a certification Dropdown, storing PG and showing “PG: parental guidance”; Translations of title and a WYSIWYG synopsis, English mandatory and Spanish optional; a Preview URL, https://reelhouse.example/{{$language}}/films/{{slug}}; a Unique set of title and release_year; genres, a Many to Many through films_genres; and cast, a Many to Many to people through films_cast, a Junction collection with Fields of its own, the character played and the billing_order. Its Display template, the title and the year of its release date, reads “Paper Comets (2019)”.
tv_shows Field groups, Overview, Artwork and Seasons and cast, the last collapsed at first; a status Dropdown whose Field condition hides end_year until the show has ended; seasons, a One to Many; a cast of its own, as films have; and the primary Action “Count episodes”.
seasons A Many to One to its TV show, tv_show, and a One to Many of its episodes; a Unique set of tv_show and number; a Display template mixing text and a Field, “Season 2”.
episodes A Many to One to its season; release_dates, a Repeater of the date it aired in each country.
genres An Icon icon and a Colour colour Field.
people An Image photo, in the Photos File folder, a born date and a biography.
characters The simplest Collection: a name and a description, linked from each cast link.
homepage A singleton, with a single Item and no List view; featured, a Many to Any to films and TV shows.
languages The Content languages collection, with its code (en, es), mandatory and default Fields.

Around the model:

  • Roles: Admin; Editor, who edits the whole catalogue but not its languages; Translator, who changes nothing but the films’ Translations; and Viewer, who reads everything. Editors and Viewers may run “Count episodes”.
  • File folders: Posters and Photos, which the Editor may view, replace and delete in.
  • An image server: the demo runs a stand-in for Reelhouse’s, which answers each film’s still, a band of its poster, and its previews; it points the films’ still at it and lists its origin in NEXUS_IMAGE_ORIGINS.
  • A video player: the demo runs a stand-in for Reelhouse’s, the Video site reelhouse-player, which answers each film’s trailer, a still frame that never plays, and its thumbnail; it points the films’ trailer at it and lists its origin in NEXUS_VIDEO_ORIGINS.
  • Two Webhooks: “Film published”, which tells Reelhouse’s listings service each time a film is added or changed, and “Rebuild the website”, inactive, that would call https://reelhouse.example/hooks/rebuild whenever a film, TV show, season, episode or the homepage changes. The demo runs a stand-in listings service, which Film published calls, down once for Brass Orchard and failing Velvet Static every time, so that its Delivery log shows calls retried and failed.
  • The Extension cinema-demo, whose Extension hooks trim a film’s title and collapse its spaces before it’s written, keep films from API users until the year they’re released, and log each film added, and whose Action handler count-episodes runs behind “Count episodes”.
  • The Read model, kept by every Collection a website would read.
  • Users: its admin, admin@reelhouse.example; Marta Quiroga and Owen Lark, Editors; Jordi Bassols, its Translator; Hannah Pryce, a Viewer since deactivated; and two people invited, as an Editor and a Translator. Their emails are their first names at reelhouse.example.
  • An API user, “Website build”, a Viewer, with the API tokens Production and Staging.
  • A shared Saved view, “Latest releases”, the films with the latest released first, which its admin shares with the Editor and Translator Roles, and the admin’s own, “Saturday premieres”, the films that opened on a Saturday, by the weekday of their release date.
  • Marta’s work: once signed in, she corrects The Saltmarsh Inheritance’s runtime and runs “Count episodes” on Wexmoor Tide.
  • Project settings branded Reelhouse: its name, a red main colour, the Manrope typography and its own logo.

Running it

Section titled “Running it”

You need a checkout of the Nexus repository with its dependencies installed (pnpm install), and Docker, which runs its database. Then, from the repository’s root:

It builds Nexus and the cinema-demo Extension, starts an empty Nexus with a throwaway PostgreSQL container, applies Reelhouse’s Model snapshot, seeds its Items, brands it and adds its Users, then says where to sign in:

  1. Open http://localhost:3100.
  2. Choose Sign in with Google. The demo doesn’t reach Google: a stand-in identity issuer shows a form instead. Sign in with Microsoft leads to the same form, as Reelhouse’s other Identity provider.
  3. Enter admin@reelhouse.example and sign in. You’re Reelhouse’s admin, in the Content section.

To see Nexus as an Editor or the Translator, sign out and sign in again at the same form as marta@reelhouse.example or jordi@reelhouse.example. Its Invitations are sent to a stand-in mail server, which keeps them to itself.

Ctrl+C stops it and throws its database and Files away, so each run starts from the same Reelhouse. If something else already uses port 3100, 4410, the stand-in issuer’s, or 4412, the stand-in listings service’s, set REELHOUSE_PORT, REELHOUSE_ISSUER_PORT and REELHOUSE_LISTINGS_PORT to others.

Tip

Change anything you like: nothing you do in the demo outlives it. To start again, stop it and run pnpm demo once more.
