Choosing what's published

Each Collection says what it gives the Read model, in the Read model tab of its page in the Data model section, for admins: whether it keeps one, which of its Fields go into it, which of its Translations’ Fields, and which of those its clients may sort and filter by. Saved with the tick in the header, or Ctrl+S.

Keeping one

Section titled “Keeping one”

Keep a Read model puts the Collection’s Items into it, each an entry, or takes them out. A new Collection keeps one, of its id alone, until an admin chooses more. Every Collection of Reelhouse that its website would read keeps one; its Content languages collection, languages, doesn’t.

Turning it on brings every Item in, each as of its own last change. Turning it off takes them out, and with them the Relations of other Collections that lead to it alone: no Relation leads to an entry that isn’t there. Its Items stay as they are in the CMS either way.

Its Fields

Section titled “Its Fields”

Fields lists those that can go into it, each with In the Read model. The id always does. Of the rest:

  • Every Field that stores a value can: a text, a number, a date, a Dropdown, a Repeater, a File or Image Field, and the System fields saying when and by whom an Item was created and updated. Presentation Fields store none.
  • A Relation can when the Collection it leads to keeps a Read model too, a Many to Any’s when any of its Collections does. It holds the ids of the entries it leads to, which a client may include in their place.
  • Translations aren’t a Relation of the Read model: the Fields it translates are chosen apart, below.

A Field that isn’t chosen isn’t in the Read model at all: nothing a client may query reveals it. Films keeps its title, slug, release_year, runtime, certification, poster, genres and cast; a Field the website has no use for, or that editors alone should see, stays out.

Translated fields

Section titled “Translated fields”

A Collection with Translations chooses, under Translated fields, which Fields of its Translations collection go into it: those that store a value and its Relations, on the same terms as its own, but for the Translations collection’s id and its parent and language. They’re merged into the entry once per Content language, a translated Field taking the place of the Collection’s own of the same key. Films translates its title and synopsis, and its voice_cast, the people who dub it in each Content language.

A translated Relation holds what an own one would, the ids of the entries it leads to, in the Content language queried, and a client includes them as it would an own one’s. An entry without a translation in that language leaves it out, as it does any translated Field. A Collection can’t choose an own Field and a translated one of the same key when either is a Relation: saving is refused, as a client couldn’t tell which one include loads.

A Translations collection has no Read model of its own: its tab says so, and names the parent whose tab chooses its Fields.

Sorting and filtering by

Section titled “Sorting and filtering by”

Sort and filter marks a chosen Field for clients to query by, sort and filter. Each Field marked is held in an indexed column too, so marking a Field a client never queries by costs a column for nothing. Every entry is sorted and filtered by its id, its Collection and when it last changed, whatever its Collection marks: updated_on can’t be marked, as the entry has its own.

Only a Field an indexed column can hold may be marked: a text, a number, a date or time, a boolean, a UUID, a Dropdown, a Many to One, by the id of the entry it leads to, and a One to Many, Many to Many or Many to Any, by the ids of the entries it links. A many-relation is filtered by them but never sorted, as the tooltip of the info icon beside its checkbox says. One that holds JSON, a CSV list, a Repeater’s Rows or a File’s metadata can’t be marked; it shows Not available.

A many-relation’s column holds the ids of the entries it links, even when its Junction collection keeps its links (below) and its entries hold the links’ ids; a Many to Any’s, only those of its Collections that keep a Read model.

Reelhouse’s films are sorted and filtered by their slug, release_year and certification, and by their translated title, and filtered by their genres and cast; genres and people by their name, TV shows by their status and their start_year, as release_year. A film’s runtime is in its entry, but a query can’t filter by it.

Two Collections whose marked Fields share a key and a definition share the column: a query across Collections, such as genres and people by name, filters both at once. Many-relations of one key share one too, whatever Collections they lead to.

Query keys

Section titled “Query keys”

A Field marked is sorted and filtered by its Query key: its own key, the placeholder of the Query key box beside it, unless you type another. Fields of different Collections given one Query key share its column, own or translated alike, so a single query sorts and filters them together. Reelhouse gives TV shows’ start_year the Query key release_year, so films and TV shows are listed together, the newest first, by -release_year (Querying).

The box lists, as you type or press the down arrow, the Query keys the other Collections that keep a Read model already use for Fields of the same kind of value, on the same side, own or translated, as their saved settings stand, and takes a new one too. The tab reads them all at once when it opens. A query then names the Field by its Query key alone: a TV show’s start_year is refused, though its entry still holds it at start_year. A Field no longer marked loses its Query key.

A Query key is refused, under its box, when:

  • another Collection sorts and filters a Field holding another kind of value by it, such as a text where TV shows have a number (incompatible-alias): pick one whose Fields hold the same kind, or change a Field’s Data type first;
  • it is id, collection or updated_on, which every entry is sorted and filtered by, or the key or Query key of another Field of the Collection marked on the same side, or the key of a translated Field marked under another Query key, or the reverse; and when a translated Field of the Field’s own key is marked under another Query key, or the reverse, as entries merge the two (alias-taken).

An own Field and a translated Field of one Query key are merged as Fields of one key are: an entry is sorted and filtered by its translation’s value in the Content language queried, when its translation has it, else by its own.

Junction collections

Section titled “Junction collections”

A Junction collection has no switch of its own. While its owner keeps a Read model and it has Fields of its own, its links are entries of their own, with those Fields and related, the Item each links, and the Relation whose links it holds holds their ids instead of the linked Items’. Without Fields of its own, the Relation holds the linked Items’ ids, and it keeps no entries. Its tab says which.

So Reelhouse’s films_cast, whose links say the character played and the billing_order, keeps its links: a film’s cast holds them, and the person each links is one level further. films_genres, which describes nothing, keeps none: a film’s genres holds the genres. See Including relations.

A Translations collection’s Junction collection follows the parent, as the Translations collection keeps no Read model of its own: films_translations_voice_cast, whose links say the character each person dubs, keeps its links while Films keeps a Read model.

Adding a Junction collection’s first Field of its own, or deleting its last, changes the shape of its owner’s entries, which Nexus warns about first.

What a change rebuilds

Section titled “What a change rebuilds”

A change to what a Collection gives the Read model rebuilds every entry of the Collection, as of its Items’ last change, so their dates don’t move: choosing a Field or no longer choosing it, marking one, giving it a Query key or changing it, which moves its values to the column of its new Query key, or changing a chosen Field’s Data type, its Relation, or deleting it. A Field no longer chosen is no longer marked, nor one whose new Data type can’t be. On a big Collection, the Read model falls behind for a while; see Rebuilds and the queue.

Clients see the new shape once their entries are rebuilt: a Field taken out is gone from them, and a query that filters by a Field no longer marked is refused.

In Model snapshots and through the API

Section titled “In Model snapshots and through the API”

A Model snapshot holds each Collection’s Read model settings, and applying it to another Project rebuilds the entries of the Collections whose settings it changes.

Through the API, a Collection’s readModel, at /api/model/collections/<key>, says whether it’s kept, its chosen fields and translatedFields, those filterable and translatedFilterable, by key, and their Query keys, aliases and translatedAliases, by Field key, a Field left out sorted and filtered by its own; a PATCH changes those it names, for the Admin role only. GET /api/model/query-keys, for the Admin role only too, answers every Query key those Collections use, at once: for each Field marked, its collection (the parent, for a translated one), its field key, its Query key, whether it’s translated, and the definition of the column that holds it, such as varchar(255), date, text for a Many to One or text[] for a many-relation.
