Collections

A Collection is a type of content: Reelhouse’s films, TV shows, people, genres. Each has its own Fields and Relations, and its own table in the Project’s database, where each of its Items is a row. Admins create and change Collections in the Data model section while Nexus runs; nothing is deployed, and editors see the change as soon as it’s saved.

Creating a Collection

Section titled “Creating a Collection”

The + button above the Navigation tree of the Data model section opens New Collection, which asks for the two things that never change:

  • Key: the Collection key, in snake_case, such as films or tv_shows: lower-case words of letters and digits joined by single underscores, starting with a letter, of 50 characters at most. It names the Collection’s table and its route in the API (/api/items/films), and can’t start with nexus_, which Nexus keeps for its own tables.
  • Primary key: how its Items are identified. UUID v7, the default, is generated by Nexus and unique everywhere; Auto-increment integer numbers them 1, 2, 3 in the database, as every Reelhouse Collection does; Manual string is typed by editors, such as a country code.

The new Collection has its System fields, a Display template of its id, a Read model holding its id alone and History kept for ever, and hangs at the top of the Navigation tree. It opens on its Fields page, ready for its first Field.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”

A Collection’s Settings page holds everything about it but its Fields, saved all at once with the button at its top right or Ctrl+S.

Identity and texts

Section titled “Identity and texts”

Identity shows the key and the primary key, which can’t change. Texts holds the Collection’s Labels: its Name, which people see instead of its key, and its Description, what it holds for those who work with it. Each is given in as many languages as you like, from the ISO 639-1 list, and each User sees it in their UI language. A User whose language has no Label sees the key as words (tv_shows reads “Tv shows”), while the Data model section always shows the key as it is. Reelhouse names its films “Films” in English and “Películas” in Spanish.

Navigation

Section titled “Navigation”
  • Hangs from: where it stands in the Navigation tree: a Folder, another Collection, or the top level. It goes at the end of what hangs there, taking what hangs from it along.
  • Icon and Colour: a Material Symbol shown beside its name wherever it’s listed, drawn in a colour of your choice. Without an icon, a table shows, or a page for a singleton. Reelhouse’s films show the movie symbol in red; its genres, category in purple.
  • Hidden: left out of the Content section, where editors no longer find it, but still reachable through Relations and the API. The Data model section keeps showing it, marked with a crossed-out eye, while Show hidden Collections is on. Translations collections and Junction collections are hidden when created.
  • Singleton: holds a single Item, which opens straight away instead of a List view, as Reelhouse’s homepage does. A Collection holding more than one Item can’t become one.

History and preview

Section titled “History and preview”

History decides whether every change to its Items is recorded, and for how many months, from 1 to 1200, or for ever; see History. Preview holds its Preview URL.

Display template

Section titled “Display template”

The Display template is how one of its Items shows wherever it appears through a Relation: in a Many to One’s Relation picker, in a list of related Items, in a List view’s column of a Relation. It’s an ordered mix of Fields and literal texts, at least one of them a Field that stores a value, of up to 20 parts, written in one Template box: texts are typed as they are, and each Field shows in it as a chip, by its name, removed whole. Insert field, the button at its end, or two opening braces, {{, typed where the Field goes, open the list of its Fields to insert, searched by name or key; Escape closes it and leaves the braces as typed. Below it, a preview shows the Collection’s first Item through it, or each Field’s name while it holds none.

Fields of related Items

Section titled “Fields of related Items”

A Display template may also show a Field of an Item that the Collection’s Many to One Relations reach, through up to three of them in a chain. In the list, each Many to One is a group, marked with an arrow, that opens on its target’s Fields and on that Collection’s own Many to One Relations, down to the third; Back returns to where it was opened. Searching finds them by every name on the way: person name finds Person › Name. Its chip is named after the whole way, and is written in the template as {{related.name}}. Only Many to One Relations lead on, and Translations to their own Fields (see Fields of Translations): One to Many, Many to Many and Many to Any Relations don’t.

Where a Relation on the way is empty, that part shows nothing, as an empty Field does. Wherever the Item shows, in the app, the API’s $display or a CSV export, the related Item’s value is read as it is now: a change of the related Item changes the Item’s Display text. The Read model holds no Display text: its clients compose what they show from the Fields they chose.

A date’s parts

Section titled “A date’s parts”

A date, datetime, timestamp or time Field may show one of its parts instead of its value: its year, its month, its weekday… In the list, such a Field is a group, marked with an arrow, that opens on its Value, the date as its Interface writes it, and on the parts its Data type has, Year, Quarter, Month, Week, Day, Weekday, Hour, Minute and Second, those of a Field function. A part’s chip is named after the Field and the part, Release date › Year, and is written in the template as {{year(released_on)}}; a related Item’s Field takes one the same way, {{year(related.born)}} for the year a cast link’s person was born in. Reelhouse’s films show as title + “ (” + year(released_on) + “)”, “Paper Comets (2019)”.

A part shows as a column of it does: a month and a weekday by name, September or septiembre, in your UI language, the other parts as numbers, a timestamp’s in your Profile’s timezone. The API’s $display writes them in the UI language of the User whose token calls, English without one, and a timestamp’s in the request’s timezone, UTC without one. A count is no part of a Display template.

The part must be one its Field’s Data type has: changing the Data type of a Field into one without a part a Display template shows, a date shown by its hour or a date into a string, is refused, as long as the template shows it. A part of a related Item’s Field that its Data type change takes away is dropped from the template instead, as one whose Field is deleted.

Fields of Translations

Section titled “Fields of Translations”

A Collection with Translations may show the Fields of its Translations too, in the Content language its Display text is read in. In the list, its Translations field is a group, marked with an arrow, that opens on its Translations collection’s Fields, but the links to the Item and to its language; its chip is named after the whole way, Translations › Title, and is written in the template as {{translations.title}}. Shown by {{translations.title}}, a Reelhouse film is Paper Comets in English and Cometas de papel in Spanish. A Translations field may also come last in a chain of Many to One Relations, within the three steps, such as {{film.translations.title}}; nothing leads on from a Translation.

The language is the one each place reads Translations in: a List view’s, from its Content language menu, the picker’s, and the List view’s of the Item’s Collection in its Item view, for its title and its Relations’ Items; the export’s in a CSV export; and the request’s language, else the default one, in the API’s $display. Where the Item has no Translation in that language, that part shows nothing, as an empty Field does, without falling back to the default language’s. Deleting a Field of the Translations collection, or the Translations field, takes its parts out of every template that shows them; deleting a Content language changes none.

Reelhouse’s Display templates:

Collection Display template Shows
films title + “ (” + year(released_on) + “)” Paper Comets (2019)
films_cast related.name + “ as “ + character.name Greta Solberg-Hale as Philippa Marsh
seasons “Season “ + number Season 2
episodes number + “. “ + title 3. A Lantern for Ada
people, characters, genres name Imogen Treadwell

A new Collection’s Display template is its id. Deleting a Field takes it out of the template, and once no Field is left the template is the id again; so does deleting a Field or a Relation on the way to a related Item’s Field, in this Collection or another. A Relation Field may show its Items through a template of its own instead, which may reach related Items’ Fields and their Translations’ too; see Relations.

Unique sets

Section titled “Unique sets”

A Unique set is two or more Fields whose values no two Items may share all at once, which the database enforces. Reelhouse defines three of its own, besides those Nexus keeps for its Junction and Translations collections: no two films share their title and release_year, as a remake has a year of its own; no TV show has two seasons of the same number; no season two episodes of the same number. An Item missing a value in any Field of a set clashes with none.

Add unique set takes the Fields of the new set. A set that Items already break is refused, listing the Items in the way, so that they can be fixed first. A single Field whose values must all differ doesn’t need a set: it is Unique itself; see Fields.

Searchable fields

Section titled “Searchable fields”

The general search of a List view looks in a Collection’s Searchable fields: a Field is one when its Searchable setting, on its General tab, is on. Only text Fields (string, text, csv) and number Fields (integer, bigint, decimal) of the Collection’s own table may be, and texts are searched for what they contain while numbers must match. The Fields of its Translations collection may be Searchable fields too, searched in the Content language the List view shows. No other Relation is searched: editors reach those through each Field’s filters.

Searching Reelhouse’s films looks in each film’s original title and, in the Content language chosen, in its translated title.

Duplicating a Collection

Section titled “Duplicating a Collection”

The copy button at the top right of a Collection’s Settings page duplicates it under a new key. The copy gets its Fields, Field groups, Field conditions and settings, and a copy of its Translations collection, if it has one. Copy its Items too copies them as well, with their Translations and links, created by you, now.

The copy doesn’t get the One to Many Fields of other Collections that lead to the original, nor the permissions Roles have on it: give Roles their Collection permissions on the copy. The dialog says which of its own One to Many Fields the copy leaves out, those whose Many to One doesn’t lead to it.

Deleting a Collection

Section titled “Deleting a Collection”

The bin beside it deletes the Collection, once its key is typed to confirm. Its table is dropped with every Item it holds, and what hangs from it in the Navigation tree takes its place. It can’t be undone.

A Collection can’t be deleted while another Collection’s Relation leads to it: delete that Field first. Nor can the Content languages collection while the Project settings designate it, nor a Junction collection on its own, which goes with its Relation.
