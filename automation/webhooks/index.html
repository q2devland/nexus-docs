Webhooks

A Webhook is a call Nexus makes to a URL of yours after Items of chosen Collections are created, updated or deleted: to rebuild a website, refresh a search index, tell another system. It says which Item changed, how and by whom, but never carries the Item itself: the receiver reads it through the API, as its own API user may. Every call is signed, so that the receiver can tell it comes from Nexus, and one that fails is retried.

Reelhouse has two. Film published tells its listings service each time a film is added to the catalogue or changed; Rebuild the website, inactive, would rebuild its website after any change to what the website shows.

The Webhooks page

Section titled “The Webhooks page”

Webhooks, in Settings, lists them, for admins only: each with its name, its events and whether it’s Active. They’re searched by name. Add Webhook, the + in the header, opens a new one’s page.

Its page

Section titled “Its page”

A Webhook’s page has three parts, saved with the tick in the header, or Ctrl+S; the bin beside it deletes it.

Call: where it calls, and how.

  • Name: what it’s for, such as the system it calls, up to 100 characters, which no other Webhook has.
  • URL: an http:// or https:// URL, up to 2,000 characters.
  • Method: POST, unless the receiver wants another. POST, PUT and PATCH send the payload as the body; GET, DELETE, HEAD and OPTIONS send only the headers.
  • Active: an inactive Webhook isn’t called, and its calls still waiting fail. Reelhouse’s Rebuild the website is, until its website takes calls.

When: what it follows.

  • Events: it’s called after an Item is Created, Updated or Deleted, one at least.
  • Collections: only changes to the Items of these Collections call it, one at least.

Delivery: how its calls are made.

  • Retries: how many more times a failed call is made, 0 to 10; 2 unless you say otherwise. See Retries.
  • Signing secret: what its calls are signed with, 16 to 200 characters. Generate makes one. Copy it to the receiver before saving: once saved, it’s kept encrypted and never shown again. Typing or generating another replaces it.
  • Headers: sent with every call, up to 20, such as one the receiver authenticates calls with. A Secret header’s value is kept encrypted and never shown again; left as it is, it keeps its value.

See its deliveries, at the top, opens the Delivery log of its calls alone.

Deleting a Webhook, once confirmed, stops it at once: its calls still waiting fail, and the Delivery log keeps those it made.

When it’s called

Section titled “When it’s called”

A Webhook is called once for each Item that’s created, updated or deleted, of its Collections and events, however Nexus is asked to: in the Item view, through the API, or by an Extension writing Items. One save that changes several Items calls it for each. An Item is updated, too, when a One to Many of another Item links it, unlinks it or moves it, as its Many to One changes.

It’s called after the change is committed, never for one rolled back, and in the background: saving an Item never waits for a receiver, nor fails because of one. A call is kept with the change in the same transaction, so that Nexus calls it once it’s back even if it stopped in between.

It isn’t called for changes Nexus doesn’t make: those made straight in the database, an Extension’s own SQL among them, or the Items the database deletes in cascade when an Item they belong to is deleted.

The payload

Section titled “The payload”

A call with a body sends JSON, Content-Type: application/json:

That’s Film published, after Marta Quiroga, an Editor, corrected The Saltmarsh Inheritance’s runtime.

  • delivery: its entry in the Delivery log, the same in every retry of the call.
  • event: create, update or delete.
  • collection and item: the Collection’s key and the Item’s id, as text.
  • user: who changed it, null when no User did; an API user has a name and no email.
  • on: when it changed.
  • changes, of an update alone: the Item’s own Fields that changed, before and after, as the API answers them. A change to its Relations or Translations alone leaves it empty.

A receiver that needs the Item reads it through the API by its Collection and id; one deleted can’t be read any more.

Headers

Section titled “Headers”

Every call carries, besides the Webhook’s own headers:

Header What
X-Nexus-Event create, update or delete
X-Nexus-Delivery Its entry in the Delivery log, as delivery in the payload
X-Nexus-Timestamp When it was signed, in seconds since 1970
X-Nexus-Signature sha256= and the HMAC-SHA256 of the timestamp, a full stop and the body, in hex

A header of the Webhook can’t be one of these, nor Content-Type or any other that the connection sets, such as Host or User-Agent.

Checking the signature

Section titled “Checking the signature”

The receiver signs what it receives as Nexus did, with the signing secret, and turns away the call unless both signatures match:

  1. Take X-Nexus-Timestamp and the body, as received, before parsing it; a call without a body is signed over an empty one.
  2. Compute the HMAC-SHA256 of <timestamp>.<body> under the signing secret, in hex, and put sha256= before it.
  3. Compare it with X-Nexus-Signature, in constant time.
  4. Turn away a timestamp more than a few minutes old, so that a call recorded once can’t be replayed.

Reelhouse’s listings service, in Node.js:

Answer a call quickly, with any 2xx, and do the work afterwards: Nexus waits for the answer for NEXUS_WEBHOOK_TIMEOUT, 10 seconds unless set, and a call it waited too long for fails. As a call may come more than once, such as when its answer was lost, a receiver that mustn’t do the work twice keeps the delivery ids it has done.

Retries

Section titled “Retries”

A call fails when the receiver answers anything but a 2xx, doesn’t answer within NEXUS_WEBHOOK_TIMEOUT (10 seconds unless set), or can’t be reached. Nexus then makes it again, as many times as the Webhook’s Retries say: the first after NEXUS_WEBHOOK_RETRY_DELAY (30 seconds unless set), each next one twice as long after the last. A Webhook with 3 retries tries a call 30 seconds, 1½ minutes and 3½ minutes after the first, then gives up: the call has failed for good.

Each try is made as the Webhook is then: its URL, method, headers and signing secret. A receiver that moved is called at its new URL from the next try on, once the Webhook is changed.

The Delivery log records every try.

In Model snapshots

Section titled “In Model snapshots”

A Model snapshot holds the Webhooks, without their signing secrets or the values of their secret headers. Applied to another Project, a Webhook it adds is given a signing secret of its own, and its secret headers no value, which the review names: give them the receiver’s before it’s called. A Webhook it changes keeps them.

The signing secrets and secret headers are kept encrypted under the deployment’s NEXUS_ENCRYPTION_KEY, its NEXUS_JWT_SECRET when not set. Changing it loses them: the calls of a Webhook whose secrets can’t be read fail, until it’s given them again.

Through the API

Section titled “Through the API”

/api/webhooks lists the Webhooks and adds one; /api/webhooks/<id> reads, changes and deletes one; for the Admin role only. What the page asks for is what they take, signingSecret among them.
